#### Oral Aspects
- **Storytelling Tradition**: Oral storytelling involves the use of voice and gestures to convey stories. This tradition is ancient and universal, existing in every culture to entertain, inform, and pass down cultural values and traditions. For example, Native Hawaiian storytelling includes _moʻolelo_ (stories) that are passed down orally, often accompanied by _mele_ (songs), _oli_ (chants), and _hula_ (dance), to educate the young and preserve history[](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/storytelling-and-cultural-traditions/)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_storytelling).
- **Narrative Structure**: Oral histories often follow a narrative structure that includes personal experiences, cultural myths, legends, and historical accounts. This structure allows the storyteller to frame their unique account of past events, providing a voice to those whose stories might otherwise be marginalized[](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/15995/2/Goodchild%20storytelling%20as%20oral%20history%20%28AM%29.pdf).
#### Aural Aspects
- **Vocal Qualities**: The aural aspect of oral histories involves the vocal characteristics of the storyteller, such as pitch, tone, inflection, and rhythm. These vocal elements can convey emotions, create emphasis, and enhance the listener's understanding of the story. For example, inflection and pitch variations can indicate questions, excitement, or sadness, adding depth to the narrative[](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/publicspeakingprinciples/chapter/chapter-12-vocal-aspects-of-delivery/).
- **Soundscapes**: In some storytelling traditions, ambient sounds and background noises are integral to the storytelling experience. These sounds can create a sense of place and time, enhancing the listener's immersion in the story. For instance, the sound of a crackling fire during a storytelling session can evoke a communal and intimate atmosphere[](https://filmustage.com/blog/the-role-of-sound-design/)[](https://lbbonline.com/news/how-to-tell-a-story-with-sound-design).
#### Sonic Aspects
- **Performance Elements**: Oral histories can be performative, with storytellers using expressive gestures, facial expressions, and body movements to bring the story to life. This performative nature can make oral histories suitable for adaptation into theatrical performances, where the storyteller's voice and movements contribute to the overall narrative[](https://muse.jhu.edu/pub/177/article/841958).
- **Musical Integration**: Some oral traditions incorporate music and rhythm as part of the storytelling process. This can include the use of chants, rhymes, and songs, which not only entertain but also help in memorizing and passing down stories across generations. For example, the griots of West Africa use music to accompany their storytelling, serving as both historians and entertainers[](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/storytelling-and-cultural-traditions/)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_storytelling).
